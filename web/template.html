Ten boxes. One guess.

A small convolutional network, written and trained in C++ with LibTorch on two CPU cores, files 32-pixel photos into ten pigeonholes: airplane, automobile, bird, cat, deer, dog, frog, horse, ship, truck. It never saw these photos while training. Post one from the tray, or one of your own, and watch where it goes. Photos in the wrong box carry a red tag with what they really are.

    What it can't do

    Your own photos are harder than the test set: CIFAR photos are centred, tightly framed and tiny, and a phone photo shrunk to 32×32 often isn't. It only knows ten kinds, so a photo of anything else still lands in one of the ten boxes.
